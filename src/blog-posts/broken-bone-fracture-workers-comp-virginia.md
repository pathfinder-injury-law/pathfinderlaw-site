A broken bone from an accident at work is covered by Virginia workers’ comp. The insurer is required to pay for reasonable, necessary, causally related medical care ([Va. Code § 65.2-603](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-603/)), two-thirds of your average weekly wage while a doctor keeps you out of work ([Va. Code § 65.2-500](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter5/section65.2-500/)), and permanent partial disability benefits if the fracture leaves a lasting loss of use of an arm, leg, hand, foot, or other body part listed in the statute ([Va. Code § 65.2-503](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter5/section65.2-503/)). A fracture is usually easy to prove happened. The fights come later: how long you’re out, what the doctor rates, and whether problems years later are tied to the break.

## What Virginia Workers’ Comp Pays for a Broken Bone

**Comp pays several kinds of benefits for a fracture, and none of them is pain and suffering.**

| Benefit | What it covers | Law |
|---|---|---|
| Medical care | ER, casting, surgery, hardware, therapy, prescriptions, and later care tied to the break | [§ 65.2-603](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-603/) |
| Temporary total disability | Two-thirds of your average weekly wage while you can’t work at all. For accidents from July 1, 2026, through June 30, 2027, the maximum is $1,507.01 a week ([Commission rate notice](https://workcomp.virginia.gov/news/notice-of-2026-rates)) | [§ 65.2-500](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter5/section65.2-500/) |
| Temporary partial disability | Two-thirds of the difference between your old average weekly wage and what you earn on light duty or other lower-paying work | [Va. Code § 65.2-502](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter5/section65.2-502/) |
| Permanent partial disability | A set number of weeks for permanent loss of use of a listed body part. The doctor usually rates it once you reach maximum medical improvement, and it’s paid after temporary total benefits end | [§ 65.2-503](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter5/section65.2-503/) |
| Disfigurement | Up to 60 weeks for severely marked disfigurement, such as bad scarring, that isn’t already paid for under the permanent loss schedule | [§ 65.2-503(B)](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter5/section65.2-503/) |

## How Each Fracture Is Rated

**Virginia pays permanent benefits by body part. The rating goes to the listed body part that lost use, which isn’t always the bone that broke** ([§ 65.2-503](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter5/section65.2-503/)).

| Broken bone | Usually rated to | Weeks for 100% loss of use |
|---|---|---|
| Upper arm, forearm, or elbow | Arm | 200 |
| Wrist | Hand or arm, depending on the rating | 150 or 200 |
| Hand bones | Hand | 150 |
| Thumb | Thumb | 60 |
| Finger | That finger | 15 to 35, depending on the finger |
| Hip or thigh | Leg | 175 |
| Knee or shin | Leg | 175 |
| Ankle | Leg, sometimes foot | 175 or 125 |
| Heel or foot bones | Foot | 125 |
| Toe | That toe | 30 for the great toe, 10 for any other toe |
| Spine | No schedule for the back itself; nerve damage may be rated as loss of use of an arm or leg | Depends on the arm or leg rating |

The weeks come straight from the statute. Which body part a break gets rated to is a pattern, not a rule. It depends on the doctor’s findings and, if there’s a dispute, the Commission.

A 20 percent loss of use of the leg, for example, is 20 percent of 175 weeks, or 35 weeks of pay at your compensation rate. Here’s [how impairment ratings work in Virginia](/impairment-rating-virginia-workers-comp/).

## Common Work Fractures

- **Falls from ladders, roofs, and trucks.** Wrists, ankles, heels, hips, and spines break when people land. Here’s more on [broken ankles](/ankle-injury-workers-comp/), [foot fractures](/foot-injury-workers-comp/), and [hip fractures](/hurt-your-hip-at-work/).
- **Crush injuries.** Hands caught in machines and feet under loads or forklifts.
- **Struck by.** Falling objects, swinging loads, and vehicle impacts.
- **Spine fractures.** Compression fractures from falls or heavy impact.

## Where Fracture Claims Get Fought

**The break itself is rarely disputed. What it costs you over time often is.**

- **Return to work too soon.** An X-ray can show a bone healing before you can stand on it, grip with it, or lift with it all day. Light-duty offers often come before the job you actually do is possible.
- **Nonunion and hardware.** Some breaks don’t heal right. Plates and screws sometimes have to come out. That care is part of the claim.
- **Arthritis years later.** A fracture into a joint can lead to arthritis and, later, a joint replacement. Whether that care is still tied to the work injury becomes a medical question.
- **CRPS.** A small number of fractures lead to complex regional pain syndrome. Here’s more on [CRPS and spinal cord stimulators](/crps-spinal-cord-stimulator-workers-comp/).
- **Limping injuries.** Favoring a broken leg can hurt the other knee, hip, or back. That second injury can be part of the claim.
- **The rating.** The doctor’s rating drives the permanent benefit. A rating that leaves out lost motion, strength, or a second body part leaves out benefits.

## Steps After a Broken Bone at Work

1. **Report the injury to your employer in writing** right away, and no later than 30 days after the accident ([Va. Code § 65.2-600](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-600/)). A late notice can bar benefits unless the Commission accepts your reason for the delay and finds the employer wasn’t harmed by it.
2. **Get treatment and tell every provider how it happened at work.**
3. **File a claim with the Commission**, even if the insurer is paying. The deadline is generally two years from the accident ([Va. Code § 65.2-601](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-601/)).
4. **Keep every work note and restriction**, and give copies to your employer.
5. **Don’t accept a rating or a settlement until you know whether more surgery is likely.** Here’s [whether to settle before or after surgery](/settle-workers-comp-before-or-after-surgery-virginia/).

## What Is a Broken Bone Worth?

What drives the value is your average weekly wage, the weeks you’re out, the body part and the rating, whether you can go back to your old job, and the future care the break will need.

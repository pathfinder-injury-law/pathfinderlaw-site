*Nothing in this article should be taken as legal advice. Every case is different.*

If your Virginia employer was required to carry workers’ comp insurance and didn’t, you can still file a claim with the Virginia Workers’ Compensation Commission. If the Commission finds the injury is covered and the employer had no insurance, it can order your benefits paid from the Uninsured Employer’s Fund, a fund the Commission runs for exactly this situation ([Va. Code § 65.2-1203](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter12/section65.2-1203/)). The deadlines don’t change: written notice within 30 days and a claim filed within two years of the accident.

## Is Workers’ Comp Required in Virginia?

**Yes, for most employers with three or more employees.** Every employer covered by the Act has to insure its comp liability, qualify to self-insure, or belong to a licensed group self-insurance program ([Va. Code § 65.2-801](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter8/section65.2-801/)). The Act leaves out some workers and small employers ([Va. Code § 65.2-101](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter1/section65.2-101/), definition of “employee”):

| Who | Covered? |
|---|---|
| Employees of a business that regularly has three or more employees in Virginia | Yes |
| Employees of a business that regularly has fewer than three employees | Generally no, unless the employer chose to be covered |
| Farm and horticultural workers | Only if the employer regularly has more than three full-time employees |
| Domestic workers in a private home | Generally no |
| Casual employees | No |

Part-time workers count toward the three-employee number, along with full-time workers, if the business regularly uses them to do its work (a Court of Appeals decision, [32 Va. App. 77](https://www.vacourts.gov/opinions/opncavwp/0991994.pdf) (2000)). The farm rule is different: it counts only full-time employees. The question is how many people the business “regularly” has in service, which is a fact question when a business’s headcount goes up and down. If you work on a farm, here’s more on [farm and seasonal workers’ coverage](/chicken-house-and-farm-workers-comp-virginia/).

## How to Find Out if Your Employer Has Coverage

**Don’t rely on what your boss says. Check.** Ways to find out:

1. **Ask for the insurance information in writing.** The employer should be able to give you the name of its comp insurance company.
2. **Look for the required notice at work.** Covered employers have to post the Commission’s notice of coverage where employees will see it ([Commission Rule 7](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title16/agency30/chapter50/section80/)).
3. **Use the Commission’s coverage lookup.** The Virginia Workers’ Compensation Commission’s [Insurance Coverage Search](https://www.ewccv.com/cvs/) lets anyone look up an employer’s policy by business name or federal employer ID number. It shows only commercial insurance, not employers that self-insure or belong to a group self-insurance program, so “no results” doesn’t prove there’s no coverage.
4. **File a claim anyway.** Insurance companies have to file proof of each employer’s coverage with the Commission ([Va. Code § 65.2-804](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter8/section65.2-804/)), and a filed claim puts the coverage question in front of the Commission.

An employer that says “we don’t do workers’ comp” may be wrong about the law, may have coverage it doesn’t know about, or may be calling you an independent contractor. The label doesn’t decide it. Here’s [what to do when your employer lists you as an independent contractor](/what-to-do-when-your-employer-lists-you-as-an-independent-contractor/).

## What the Uninsured Employer’s Fund Pays

**The fund pays the benefits your employer’s insurance would have paid, once the Commission awards them.** That means medical treatment and wage benefits under the same rules as any other Virginia comp claim. Here’s [what workers’ comp pays in Virginia](/work-injury-compensation/).

The path looks like this:

1. **You file a claim with the Commission** and give written notice to your employer, the same as any claim.
2. **The Commission investigates.** It has to find two things: the employer failed to carry the required coverage, and your claim is compensable ([§ 65.2-1203(A)](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter12/section65.2-1203/)).
3. **The Commission can make a provisional award, then a final award,** and order payment from the fund.
4. **The Attorney General’s office may defend the fund** at the hearing, the way an insurance company would ([Va. Code § 65.2-1202](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter12/section65.2-1202/)). Expect your claim to be tested.
5. **You generally have to demand payment from the employer first.** After an award against the employer, the fund pays once the claimant has made demand on the employer, unless the Commission waives that step for good cause ([§ 65.2-1203(A)(2)](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter12/section65.2-1203/)).

After the fund pays, the Commission can go after the employer to recover the money ([Va. Code § 65.2-1204](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter12/section65.2-1204/)). The employer can also be fined up to $250 a day, to a total of $50,000, for going without coverage ([Va. Code § 65.2-805](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter8/section65.2-805/)). None of that is your job. Your job is the claim.

## Is There Another Company That Has to Pay?

**Often, yes.** If your employer was a subcontractor, the company it was working for may be your “statutory employer” and responsible for your benefits as if it had hired you directly ([Va. Code § 65.2-302](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter3/section65.2-302/)). This comes up all the time in construction, where a small crew with no insurance works under a general contractor that has it. It also comes up with staffing agencies and cleaning crews. Here’s an example from the poultry plants: [staffing agency or sanitation crew workers injured at a plant](/staffing-agency-or-sanitation-crew-injured-at-poultry-plant/).

A covered company up the chain changes the case: that company, and its insurance, can be responsible for your benefits, so the fund may not be the only place to look. Who was above whom on the job, and what work each company was doing, decides it. Write down every company name you saw on the site, the trucks, the paperwork, and your paychecks.

## Can You Sue an Employer With No Comp Insurance?

**Virginia also lets a worker sue an uninsured employer in court, and the employer loses some of its usual defenses** ([§ 65.2-805(A)](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter8/section65.2-805/)). In that lawsuit, the employer can’t argue that you were careless, that a coworker caused the injury, or that you assumed the risk. That lawsuit is a separate case with its own deadlines and rules, and it is not something Pathfinder Injury Law handles. If it may fit your situation, we may be able to point you to someone who does. The two paths are tied together. You can pursue both at the same time, but you can collect only one recovery, and once your comp claim ends in a final award you can collect, the lawsuit is generally off the table ([a 2012 Supreme Court of Virginia decision](https://www.vacourts.gov/opinions/opnscvwp/1101902.pdf)). That timing is worth sorting out early, before the comp claim reaches a final award.

## Steps to Take if Your Employer Has No Comp Insurance

1. **Get medical care** and tell every provider you were hurt at work.
2. **Give your employer written notice within 30 days** ([Va. Code § 65.2-600](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-600/)). Keep a copy.
3. **File a claim with the Commission within two years.** Here are [all the Virginia workers’ comp deadlines](/workers-comp-deadlines-virginia/).
4. **Keep proof of your job.** Pay stubs, texts from your boss, work schedules, photos of the job site, and the names of coworkers.
5. **Write down every company involved,** including the one your employer was working for.
6. **Keep your medical bills.** If the fund pays, it pays under the same rules, and you’ll need the records.

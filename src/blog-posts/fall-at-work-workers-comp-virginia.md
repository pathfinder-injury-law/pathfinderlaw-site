*Nothing in this article should be taken as legal advice. Every case is different.*

A fall at work in Virginia is covered by workers’ comp when something about the job caused it: a wet floor, a loose step, a ladder, a load you were carrying, a height you were working at. Falling while on the clock isn’t enough by itself. The law covers an injury “arising out of and in the course of the employment” ([Va. Code § 65.2-101](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter1/section65.2-101/)), and Virginia reads “arising out of” with an “actual risk” test: the job has to expose you to the danger that made you fall ([a Supreme Court of Virginia decision involving Chesterfield County, 237 Va. 180 (1989)](https://law.justia.com/cases/virginia/supreme-court/1989/871113-1.html)). A fall nobody can explain, and a fall caused only by a personal health problem, are often denied. The details of how you fell decide most fall claims.

## Which Falls Are Covered in Virginia?

**The question is always what made you fall, and whether that was a risk of the job.**

| How the fall happened | Usually covered? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Slipped on a wet, icy, or greasy floor at work | Usually yes | A condition of the workplace caused it |
| Tripped on a cord, pallet, hose, or uneven surface at work | Usually yes | Same |
| Fell from a ladder, scaffold, roof, dock, or truck | Often yes | Heights are a job risk, but you still have to show what made you fall |
| Fell while carrying, pushing, or rushing because of the job | Can be | Covered if the job duty helped cause the fall, not just because you had something in your hands |
| Fell on ordinary stairs with nothing wrong and no job duty hurrying or distracting you | Usually no | Stairs alone are a risk people face anywhere |
| Fell and nobody, including you, can say why | Usually no | The cause has to be shown, unless a presumption applies |
| Fainted, had a seizure, or a knee gave out from a personal condition, on a level floor | Usually no | The cause was personal, not the job |
| Same personal condition, but you fell from a height, into machinery or a sharp corner, or in a moving vehicle | Can be | The job made the fall more dangerous |

## Falls on Stairs

**A fall on stairs at work isn’t automatically covered in Virginia.** A water treatment plant worker turned around on the first step of a set of stairs to go back and check a meter, and his knee gave way. He didn’t slip or trip, and the steps were well lit with nothing wrong with them. The Supreme Court of Virginia held his injury did not arise out of the employment, because it could just as well have happened turning on a level floor ([a Supreme Court of Virginia decision involving Chesterfield County, 237 Va. 180 (1989)](https://law.justia.com/cases/virginia/supreme-court/1989/871113-1.html)). The Court of Appeals of Virginia puts the rule this way: the steps must be defective, or a condition of the employment must cause the fall (a Court of Appeals of Virginia decision involving the County of Buchanan School Board, 35 Va. App. 26 (2001)). What changes the answer is a defect or a job demand: a broken, slick, or unusually high step, or a job duty that hurried or distracted you on the stairs. Say so on the first report, because the insurer will ask.

## Falls Nobody Can Explain

**When a worker can’t say why they fell, Virginia generally treats the fall as unexplained, and an unexplained fall usually isn’t covered.** The worker has the burden of showing a link between the job and the fall. In one case, a truck driver remembered reaching for the grab bar on his icy cab and then waking up on the pavement. The Court of Appeals of Virginia denied the claim because nothing showed what made him fall (a Court of Appeals of Virginia decision involving PYA/Monarch, 22 Va. App. 215 (1996)).

There is one exception in the statute. The law presumes the accident arose out of and in the course of the employment when the worker (1) is physically or mentally unable to testify, as confirmed by competent medical evidence, (2) dies without ever regaining consciousness after the accident, (3) dies at or near the place of the accident, or (4) is found dead where they were reasonably expected to be for work. The facts also have to be strong enough that the only rational inference is that the accident arose out of and in the course of the job ([Va. Code § 65.2-105](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter1/section65.2-105/)). The insurer can overcome that presumption with enough evidence to the contrary.

For everyone else, what you remember matters. A worker who says “I don’t know what happened” on the first ER form often has a harder claim than one who says “my boot caught the edge of the mat.” Write down what you remember as soon as you can, and look at the spot again.

## Falls From a Health Condition

**When a personal condition causes the fall, the injury is usually not covered, unless the job made the fall more dangerous.** If you fainted and fell to a level floor, the fall came from you, not the job. But the effects of that kind of fall can be covered when the job put you on a height, near machinery or sharp corners, or in a moving vehicle (a Court of Appeals of Virginia decision involving PYA/Monarch, 22 Va. App. 215 (1996)). So if you fainted on a ladder, a loading dock, or next to running machinery, the claim may be covered. These cases turn on medical records and the exact scene.

## What a Fall at Work Can Pay

**A covered fall pays the same benefits as any Virginia comp claim:** medical care ([Va. Code § 65.2-603](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-603/)), two-thirds of your average weekly wage while you can’t work ([Va. Code § 65.2-500](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter5/section65.2-500/)), and permanent benefits for lasting loss of use ([Va. Code § 65.2-503](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter5/section65.2-503/)). For accidents from July 1, 2026, through June 30, 2027, the weekly maximum is $1,507.01 ([Commission rate notice](https://workcomp.virginia.gov/news/notice-of-2026-rates)).

## Can I Sue My Employer for a Fall at Work?

**Generally no.** In Virginia, workers’ comp is the only remedy against your employer for a work injury, whoever was at fault ([Va. Code § 65.2-307](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter3/section65.2-307/)). Your own carelessness usually doesn’t bar the claim either, though willful misconduct, willfully breaking a known safety rule, and a few other things listed in the statute can ([Va. Code § 65.2-306](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter3/section65.2-306/)). Here’s [how to qualify for workers’ comp in Virginia](/how-to-qualify-for-workers-comp-in-virginia/).

## Steps After a Fall at Work

1. **Report the fall in writing** within 30 days ([Va. Code § 65.2-600](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-600/)), and say what made you fall.
2. **Photograph the spot**, the stairs, the floor, the ladder, or the shoes you wore, before it is cleaned or fixed.
3. **Get names of anyone who saw it** or saw the condition before you fell.
4. **Tell every doctor how the fall happened.** The first medical record is often the one the insurer quotes.
5. **File a claim with the Commission** within two years of the fall ([Va. Code § 65.2-601](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-601/)). Here are [all the Virginia workers’ comp deadlines](/workers-comp-deadlines-virginia/).

*Nothing in this article should be taken as legal advice. Every case is different. Pathfinder Injury Law has no connection to Gallagher Bassett or Sedgwick.*

If Gallagher Bassett called you about a work injury, it is acting as a claims administrator, sometimes called a third-party administrator or TPA: a claims management company that employers and insurance companies hire to handle claims ([Gallagher Bassett](https://www.gallagherbassett.com/solutions/claims-management/workers-compensation/)). The adjuster who calls you works for the administrator, and the administrator works for the employer or insurer. It does not decide your rights. In Virginia, the Workers’ Compensation Commission does, and your claim with the Commission is a separate thing from the claim number the administrator gives you ([Commission glossary](https://workcomp.virginia.gov/documents/vwc-glossary-terms)). The same is true of Sedgwick and every other administrator.

## Who’s Who in a Virginia Comp Claim

**Four parties show up on the paperwork, and only the Commission decides the claim.**

| Who | What they do |
|---|---|
| Your employer | Reports the injury and is responsible for benefits under the Act |
| The insurer, or the employer itself (or its self-insurance group) if self-insured | Pays the benefits ([Va. Code § 65.2-801](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter8/section65.2-801/)) |
| The claims administrator (Gallagher Bassett, Sedgwick, and others) | Handles the claim for the employer or insurer: investigates, pays or denies, sends you letters |
| The Virginia Workers’ Compensation Commission | The state agency that decides disputes, approves settlements, and enters awards ([Va. Code § 65.2-700](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter7/section65.2-700/)) |

## What Happens After the First Call

**The administrator investigates, then accepts or denies, and the law sets the clock for some of it.**

1. **The report of injury.** Your employer, its representative, or its insurer has to report the injury to the Commission within 10 days after the injury and the employer’s knowledge of it ([Va. Code § 65.2-900](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter9/section65.2-900/)). The Commission gives the claim its own number, called a Jurisdiction Claim Number (JCN), and mails you a notice ([Commission](https://workcomp.virginia.gov/content/injured-workers)).
2. **The investigation.** Expect questions about how it happened, your job, your earlier injuries, and your doctors. The adjuster may ask for a recorded statement. Here’s [what to know before a recorded statement](/insurance-recorded-statement/).
3. **The doctor.** In Virginia, the employer generally gets to offer a panel of at least three doctors, and you pick from that list ([Va. Code § 65.2-603](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-603/)). Here’s [how choosing a doctor works](/virginia-workers-comp-doctor/).
4. **Accept or deny.** The administrator may accept the claim and start paying, or deny it in a letter. A denial letter is not a ruling. Only the Commission can decide the claim ([Va. Code § 65.2-700](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter7/section65.2-700/)).
5. **Payments.** A compensation payment not made within two weeks after it becomes due gets 20 percent added, unless the Commission finds it was paid as promptly as practicable and the delay had a good cause outside the employer’s control. A few other narrow exceptions apply ([Va. Code § 65.2-524](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter5/section65.2-524/)).

## The Administrator’s Claim Number Is Not a Claim With the Commission

**Even when the administrator accepts your claim and pays every week, you still need to file your own claim with the Commission.** The deadline is generally two years from the accident ([Va. Code § 65.2-601](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-601/)). The Commission says the same: even if your employer or its insurer has paid you voluntarily, you still need to file ([Commission](https://workcomp.virginia.gov/content/injured-workers)). Checks from the administrator don’t stop that clock. Here’s [how to file a claim](/how-to-file-a-workers-comp-claim/) and [every Virginia comp deadline](/workers-comp-deadlines-virginia/).

## What the Adjuster May Ask You For

**You have duties in a comp claim, but not every request is one you have to say yes to on the spot.**

| Request | What to know |
|---|---|
| A recorded statement | What you say can be used as evidence. Consistency with your medical records matters. |
| A medical records release | Read what it covers. Records about the injured body part are one thing; your whole history is another. |
| A visit with a nurse case manager | Ask what the nurse’s role is and who receives the nurse’s notes. |
| An exam with a doctor the insurer chose | While you claim benefits, you have to attend an exam, at a reasonable time and place, with a doctor the employer picks and pays for. Without Commission approval, the employer gets only one exam per medical specialty, you can bring your own doctor, and refusing can suspend your benefits ([Va. Code § 65.2-607](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-607/)). Here’s [how to prepare for an IME](/virginia-workers-comp-lawyer/how-to-prepare-for-an-ime-in-virginia/). |
| A light-duty job offer | Turning down a job that fits your restrictions can stop your weekly checks, unless the Commission finds the refusal was justified ([Va. Code § 65.2-510](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter5/section65.2-510/)). |

## If the Administrator Denies or Stops Your Benefits

**A denial letter or a stopped check from an administrator is a position, not a decision.**

- **If the claim was never accepted,** you can file a claim and ask the Commission for a hearing.
- **If there’s an award in place,** the employer or insurer generally can’t simply stop your checks. It has to file an application with the Commission, and you get 15 days from the filing date to respond ([Commission Rule 1](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title16/agency30/chapter50/section20/)).
- **If a doctor’s treatment is refused,** that is a medical dispute the Commission can decide.

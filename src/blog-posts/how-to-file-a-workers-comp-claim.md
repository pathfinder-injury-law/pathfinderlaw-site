Getting hurt at work is stressful. You may be missing paychecks, dealing with doctors, and wondering what to do first. One of the most important steps is making sure your Workers’ Comp claim is filed correctly and on time. This guide walks through the steps in Virginia.

## What Does It Mean to File a Claim?

In Virginia, Workers’ Compensation claims are handled by the Virginia Workers’ Compensation Commission. Your employer or its insurance company may start paying benefits without any dispute. Even so, a claim for benefits filed with the Commission is what protects your rights if the insurance company later denies, delays, or stops your benefits. Do not assume that someone else has filed a claim for you. If you are not sure whether a claim is on file, talk with a lawyer.

## Step 1: Get Medical Care and Tell Your Employer

Your health comes first. See a doctor promptly, even if the injury seems minor, and tell the provider that your injury happened at work. Your employer must offer a panel of at least three doctors, and you choose one.

The Virginia Workers’ Compensation Act requires [notice to your employer within thirty days of the accident](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-600/), although exceptions exist. Give written notice if at all possible. Verbal notice is OK, but much harder to prove. Include not only that an accident happened, but also that you suffered an injury and how it occurred. Keep a copy of what you send.

## Step 2: Know Your Deadline

Claims for benefits must generally be filed with the Commission within two years of the date of your accident, or else you can forever lose benefit entitlement. Exceptions exist, but they are narrow and hard to count on. Do not wait until the end of that period. You can read more about [filing deadlines](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-601/) in the Virginia Code, and a lawyer can tell you how the deadline applies to your situation.

## Step 3: Gather Your Information

Before you fill out a claim form, collect what you can. Having these items in one place makes the form easier to complete:

- Your full name, address, and contact information
- Your employer’s name and address, and your job title
- The date, time, and place of the accident
- A description of how the accident happened and which parts of your body were hurt
- Names and contact information for any witnesses
- Your medical records and any letters or notes from your doctors
- Records of your earnings before the accident, such as pay stubs
- Photos of your injury or the place where the accident happened, if you have them

If something is missing, do not let that stop you from filing on time. Talk with a lawyer about how to handle it.

## Step 4: Complete the Claim Form

The Commission provides a claim for benefits form on its website, [workcomp.virginia.gov](https://workcomp.virginia.gov/). Fill it out carefully and make sure the information is accurate and matches your other records. The form asks which benefits you are seeking. Lost wages, medical care, and permanent loss of use of part of your body are different types of benefits, and a lawyer can help you decide what to list for your situation.

## Step 5: Send It to the Commission

You can mail a completed claim form to the Virginia Workers’ Compensation Commission, 333 E. Franklin St., Richmond, VA 23219, or fax it to 804-823-6956. The Commission’s website explains other ways to file. Confirm the current address, fax number, and filing options with the Commission before you send anything, and keep a copy of everything you file along with proof of when you sent it.

## What Happens After You File?

Once the Commission has your claim, the employer and insurance company generally have the chance to respond. They may accept the claim, deny it, or say they are still investigating. You can read more about [what happens when you file a Workers’ Comp claim](/what-happens-when-you-file-a-workers-comp-claim/).

If your claim is denied, you may be able to request a hearing before the Commission. Read about [what happens if your claim is denied](/virginia-workers-comp-lawyer/what-happens-if-your-workers-comp-claim-is-denied/) and [how to appeal a denial](/how-to-appeal-a-workers-comp-denial-in-virginia/). Decisions of the Commission may be reviewed by the Court of Appeals of Virginia, and in some cases the Supreme Court of Virginia. These steps have strict deadlines, so talk with a lawyer promptly if you receive a denial.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Waiting too long to give notice or to file the claim
- Leaving out injuries or body parts that were hurt in the accident
- Guessing at dates or details instead of checking your records
- Signing forms or giving a recorded statement to the insurance company before you understand them. See [how to avoid adjusters’ tricks](/how-to-avoid-work-comp-adjusters-tricks/).
- Assuming your employer will take care of everything

## Do You Need a Lawyer to File?

You are allowed to file a claim on your own. A lawyer is not required, but many people choose to talk with one, especially if the claim has been denied, the insurance company is not responding, or your employer is disputing what happened. A lawyer can review your claim form, help you gather records, and explain your options. Attorney fees in Workers’ Comp cases are subject to the approval and award of the Commission, so ask about fees and costs at your consultation.

*Nothing in this article should be taken as legal advice. Every case is different.*

**To qualify for workers’ comp in Virginia, you have to be an employee of a covered employer, be hurt in an accident that arose out of and in the course of your job, give your employer notice within 30 days, and file a claim with the Virginia Workers’ Compensation Commission within two years of the accident.** Fault usually doesn’t matter. Each of those pieces has rules of its own, and the insurance company only needs one of them to fail to deny the claim.

| To qualify, you need | What it means | Where it trips people up |
|---|---|---|
| A covered employee | Your employer has three or more employees regularly in service in Virginia, and you work for it as an employee | Being called an “independent contractor,” or working for a subcontractor |
| An accident | A specific incident at a definite time that caused a sudden structural or mechanical change in your body | Pain that came on gradually |
| Connection to the job | The accident arose out of and in the course of your employment | Falls with no known cause, injuries on the way to work |
| Notice within 30 days | You told your employer about the accident | Telling a coworker, or waiting to see if it gets better |
| A claim within two years | You filed a claim with the Commission | Thinking the insurance company paying means a claim is on file |

## Are You a Covered Employee?

**Virtually all employers with three or more employees regularly in service in Virginia are required to carry workers’ compensation insurance** ([Va. Code § 65.2-101](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter1/section65.2-101/)). Part-time workers count, and so do you if you got hurt on your first day.

Three situations come up often:

- **You’re called an independent contractor.** The label on your paycheck doesn’t decide it. What matters most is whether the company had the right to control how you did the work. Here’s [what to do when your employer lists you as an independent contractor](/what-to-do-when-your-employer-lists-you-as-an-independent-contractor/).
- **You work for a subcontractor.** If your direct employer has no insurance, the company above it on the job may be responsible as your “statutory employer” ([Va. Code § 65.2-302](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter3/section65.2-302/)).
- **Your employer should have had insurance and didn’t.** You can still file a claim. The Commission’s Uninsured Employer’s Fund may pay benefits when an employer that was required to have coverage has none ([Va. Code § 65.2-1203](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter12/section65.2-1203/)).

## Were You Hurt in an Accident?

**Virginia workers’ comp covers injuries from an identifiable incident at a reasonably definite time that causes a sudden structural or mechanical change in your body.** You should be able to say what you were doing and when: “I was lifting a box of parts onto the top shelf and felt my shoulder pop.”

It generally does not cover injuries that build up over time from repetitive work. Pain that develops gradually is much harder to bring under workers’ comp in Virginia, even when everyone agrees the job caused it.

## Did It Arise Out of and in the Course of Your Job?

**The accident has to happen while you’re working (“in the course of”) and be caused by a risk of the job (“arising out of”).** Virginia uses an “actual risk” test: the job itself has to expose you to the danger that hurt you. A risk anyone could face anywhere, apart from work, usually isn’t enough.

That’s why the details matter so much. A fall on stairs at work isn’t automatically covered. A fall caused by a wet step, a loose handrail, or a heavy load your job had you carrying usually is. And a fall with no known cause usually isn’t covered at all, unless a legal presumption applies.

Injuries on the way to or from work are generally not covered, with exceptions, such as when your employer provides the transportation or the trip is part of your job. Here’s more on [a car accident at work in Virginia](/car-accident-at-work-in-virginia/).

## Does It Matter Whose Fault It Was?

**Fault generally doesn’t matter in Virginia workers’ compensation claims.** Even if your own mistake or negligence caused your work injury, you can usually still recover benefits. However, a willful failure to use a safety appliance or perform a statutory duty, or a willful breach of a known, reasonable rule of the employer, can bar recovery. The employer has the burden of proof ([Va. Code § 65.2-306](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter3/section65.2-306/)).

Your employer’s fault doesn’t matter either way. You don’t have to prove your employer did anything wrong.

## What Are the Deadlines?

**The Virginia Workers’ Compensation Act requires notice to your employer within thirty days of the accident, although exceptions exist** ([Va. Code § 65.2-600](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-600/)). Tell your supervisor, in writing if you can, and keep a copy. Say how it happened and every body part that hurts.

**A claim must be on file with the Virginia Workers’ Compensation Commission within two years of your date of accident, although exceptions apply** ([Va. Code § 65.2-601](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-601/)).

Reporting the injury to your employer is not the same as filing a claim. Neither is the insurance company accepting the injury and paying your doctor. File a claim with the Commission anyway, even if the insurance company is paying, because that is what protects your two-year deadline. Here’s [how to file a workers’ comp claim](/how-to-file-a-workers-comp-claim/) and [what happens after you file](/what-happens-when-you-file-a-workers-comp-claim/).

## What Do You Get If You Qualify?

- **Medical care.** The insurance company is required to pay for medical treatment that is reasonable, necessary, and causally related to your work accident. That doesn’t mean it will. You’ll usually choose a doctor from a list (a “panel”) the employer gives you. Here’s more on [choosing a doctor for a Virginia workers’ comp claim](/virginia-workers-comp-doctor/).
- **Wage checks while you can’t work.** Two-thirds of your pre-injury average weekly wage, up to a maximum weekly rate the Commission sets each year. Checks start on day 8, and the first seven days are paid too if you’re out more than three weeks ([Va. Code § 65.2-509](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter5/section65.2-509/)).
- **Partial wage checks on light duty.** If you go back at lower pay, two-thirds of the difference. Here’s more on [light duty in Virginia](/light-duty-workers-comp-virginia/).
- **A permanent rating.** If you’re left with permanent loss of use of a body part on Virginia’s schedule, you’re paid a set number of weeks for each percent. Here’s how an [impairment rating works in Virginia](/impairment-rating-virginia-workers-comp/).

## Why Do Claims That Qualify Still Get Denied?

Qualifying and getting paid are two different things. A few of the many defenses insurance companies raise:

- **“There was no accident.”** The first medical record says your back “has been hurting for weeks,” or your recorded statement doesn’t name a specific moment.
- **“It didn’t arise out of the job.”** The insurance company argues you’d have been hurt the same way anywhere.
- **“It’s an old injury.”** You had prior treatment for the same body part. An accident that makes an old condition worse can still be covered, but you usually need a doctor to explain how. Here’s more on [pre-existing conditions](/pre-existing-conditions-and-workers-comp-claims/).
- **“That body part was never mentioned.”** A knee or shoulder that doesn’t show up in the early medical records is hard to add later.
- **“Notice was late.”** Waiting to see if it gets better can cost you.

Most of these are decided by records written in the first days after the accident, before anyone is arguing about your claim.

## Can I Sue My Employer Instead?

**Generally, no.** Workers’ comp is the exclusive remedy against your employer, so a lawsuit against your employer is generally barred except in extremely rare situations ([Va. Code § 65.2-307](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter3/section65.2-307/)). You generally can’t sue a coworker, either.

You may have a separate claim against someone outside your company who caused the accident, such as the driver who hit your work truck or the maker of a defective machine. That claim runs alongside your workers’ comp claim, and the insurance company may have a right to be repaid from it.

## Questions About Qualifying

### Do Part-Time and Seasonal Workers Qualify?

Yes, if you’re an employee and the employer is covered. Your weekly wage checks are based on what you actually earned, so a part-time worker’s checks will be smaller.

### Do I Qualify If I Was Hurt on My First Day?

Yes. There’s no waiting period to become covered. Coverage starts when your employment does.

### Do I Qualify If I Already Had a Problem with That Body Part?

You can. The question is whether the work accident caused a new injury or made the old one worse. Expect the insurance company to ask for your prior records.

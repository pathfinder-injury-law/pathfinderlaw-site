Knees take a beating at work. Bending, kneeling, climbing, lifting, and twisting all run through one joint, and a single bad step can keep you off the job for months. If you hurt your knee at work in Virginia, workers’ compensation can pay for your medical care and part of your lost wages. Whether it does, and how much it pays, depends on how the injury happened and what it does to your ability to work.

This guide covers the knee injuries we see most, what Virginia law requires before workers’ comp pays, what you can receive, and what drives the value of a knee claim.

## Common Knee Injuries at Work

The knee is one of the largest and most complex joints in your body. Many knee injuries do not show up on an x-ray. Torn cartilage and ligaments usually need an MRI to diagnose, but that does not make them any less serious.

### Torn Meniscus and Cartilage Damage

Cartilage covers the ends of the thighbone (femur) and shinbone (tibia) and the back of the kneecap (patella). Two C-shaped pieces of cartilage, the menisci, sit between the femur and tibia. Together they let the joint glide smoothly when you bend and straighten your leg.

A meniscus tear is one of the most common knee injuries in workers’ comp claims. It often happens when you twist while your foot is planted, or when you get up quickly from a squat or kneel. Damaged cartilage can cause pain, swelling, locking, and loss of motion.

### Ligament Tears (ACL, MCL, PCL, and LCL)

Four main ligaments hold the knee together:

- The **medial collateral ligament (MCL)** on the inside and the **lateral collateral ligament (LCL)** on the outside control side-to-side movement.
- The **anterior cruciate ligament (ACL)** and **posterior cruciate ligament (PCL)** cross inside the joint and control front-to-back movement.

Any of these can tear partly or completely. ACL tears often come from a sudden stop, a pivot, or a bad landing. A complete tear often needs surgery. See [torn ACL at work](#torn-acl-at-work-surgery-time-off-and-your-claim) below.

### Broken Kneecap and Other Fractures

Fractures are one of the few knee injuries an x-ray usually shows. The kneecap is the bone most often broken, usually in a fall onto the knee or a direct blow. The ends of the femur and tibia can also break, often in falls from height or vehicle crashes.

### Tendon Injuries and Other Soft Tissue Damage

Two large tendons run across the front of the knee: the quadriceps tendon above the kneecap and the patellar tendon below it. A fall or an awkward landing can tear either one, and a full tear usually needs surgery. Other soft tissue injuries include sprains, bursitis, dislocations, and deep bruises. They can sound minor but can still limit how you walk, kneel, and climb.

### Amputation

Amputations are rare. They can happen in crush injuries, falls into machinery, and other severe accidents.

## What Virginia Workers’ Comp Requires for a Knee Injury

### Most Knee Claims Need a Specific Accident

**Virginia workers’ comp covers a knee injury caused by a specific accident at work that produces a sudden structural or mechanical change in the knee.** It generally does not cover knee problems that develop slowly from wear and tear, repetitive kneeling, or years of hard work. That is why it matters whether your knee problem is new (acute) or long-standing (chronic). Your MRI and your doctor’s notes often decide that question.

### Arthritis and Other Pre-Existing Knee Problems

Purely degenerative arthritis, even when work makes it hurt more, is generally not covered by itself. But a pre-existing condition does not automatically end your claim. If a work accident caused a new structural or mechanical change, or a structural aggravation of the old condition, that injury can still be covered. And if a serious knee injury at work later leads to arthritis in that joint, the arthritis is more likely to be covered.

Here is an example. A construction worker has arthritis in one knee. If the knee keeps getting worse on the job until the ACL gives out with no specific accident, workers’ comp likely will not cover the tear. If the worker falls from a ladder and tears the ACL, the claim may be covered even though the arthritis was already there.

These cases turn on the medical details: what the MRI shows, what your doctor writes about the cause, and what the insurance company’s doctor says about the same images.

## Steps to Take After a Knee Injury at Work

1. **Get medical care.** In an emergency, your health comes first. Tell every doctor that you hurt your knee at work and how it happened, so your records say so from the start.
2. **Give your employer written notice within 30 days.** [Virginia law](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacodefull/title65.2/chapter6/) requires you to notify your employer within 30 days of the accident. Do it in writing. Late notice can limit or even bar your benefits. Include your name and address, the date and time of the accident, where it happened, what you were doing, and what part of your body you hurt. If you also told someone in person, note who and when.
3. **File your claim with the Virginia Workers’ Compensation Commission.** Notice to your employer is not the same as a claim. The claim must generally be filed with the Commission within two years of the accident, although exceptions apply ([Va. Code § 65.2-601](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-601/)). File it even if the insurance company accepts your injury and starts paying. A filed claim protects your two-year filing deadline (the statute of limitations) if anything goes wrong later. Here’s [how to file a workers’ comp claim](/how-to-file-a-workers-comp-claim/).
4. **Watch for the insurance company’s answer.** After you file, the Commission will order your employer’s insurance company to respond to your claim within 30 days. It may accept the claim or deny it. Whatever it decides, you can ask the Commission for a hearing, including if it [delays your benefits](/what-if-a-workers-comp-insurer-delays-my-benefits/) or [denies your claim](/virginia-workers-comp-lawyer/what-happens-if-your-workers-comp-claim-is-denied/).

## What Benefits Can You Receive for a Knee Injury in Virginia?

Workers’ comp does not pay for pain and suffering. It pays for specific things: medical care, part of your lost wages, and permanent loss of use of your leg. For a full overview, see [what workers’ comp covers in Virginia](/virginia-workers-comp-lawyer/what-does-workers-comp-cover-in-virginia/).

### Medical Care

**Your employer’s insurance company must pay for medical treatment that is reasonable, necessary, and causally related to the work accident** ([Va. Code § 65.2-603](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-603/)). For a knee, that can include office visits, prescriptions, MRIs, injections, braces, physical therapy, and surgery, including knee replacement when the work injury causes the need for it. In the right circumstances, it can also include changes to your home or vehicle.

### Lost Wages

**Wage loss benefits, also called indemnity benefits, are generally two-thirds of your average weekly wage over the 52 weeks before the accident,** with exceptions and variations ([Va. Code § 65.2-500](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter5/section65.2-500/)). See [how workers’ comp benefits are calculated in Virginia](/virginia-workers-comp-lawyer/how-is-workers-comp-calculated-in-virginia/).

- **Temporary total disability (TTD)** applies when your doctor says you cannot work at all.
- **Temporary partial disability (TPD)** applies when you are back at work on light duty but earning less than before, because of fewer hours or a lower-paying job. Getting TPD can require you to look for other work to make up the difference. The rules on light-duty offers, job searches, and how your new earnings are counted are where many TPD claims go wrong.

There is also a short waiting period before wage checks start. Here’s [when Virginia workers’ comp starts paying](/virginia-workers-comp-lawyer/when-does-virginia-workers-comp-start-paying/).

### Permanent Partial Disability (PPD)

Once you reach [maximum medical improvement](/virginia-workers-comp-lawyer/what-is-maximum-medical-improvement-in-virginia-workers-comp-claims/) (MMI), meaning your doctor says you are as healed as you are going to get, you may have a permanent loss of use of your leg. A doctor gives that loss a rating, stated as a percentage. Virginia pays PPD benefits for the permanent loss of use of certain body parts, including the legs, arms, hands, feet, fingers, and toes, and for loss of vision or hearing. The amount comes from a formula based on your average weekly wage and your rating ([Va. Code § 65.2-503](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter5/section65.2-503/)). Because a knee injury is rated as loss of use of the leg, the rating has a big effect on what a knee claim is worth.

### Permanent Total Disability (PTD)

In rare, very serious cases, an injured worker loses the use of two or more of these body parts for any kind of gainful employment. Permanent total disability benefits then pay two-thirds of the pre-injury average weekly wage for life ([Va. Code § 65.2-500](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter5/section65.2-500/)). These cases are uncommon and hard-fought, and they usually turn on medical and vocational evidence about whether you can do any kind of work.

## What If You Need Knee Surgery?

Some knee injuries do not heal with rest, medication, injections, or therapy. A torn ACL, a large meniscus tear, or a ruptured tendon may need surgery, and a severe injury may lead to a knee replacement. Expect pushback: surgery is one of the most common points of dispute with the insurance company.

If the surgery is reasonable, necessary, and related to your work injury, the insurance company is required to pay for it, along with follow-up care such as physical therapy. That does not mean it will. Insurance companies often fight surgery requests. If it refuses, or your claim has been denied, you can ask the Commission for a hearing. Your doctor’s written opinion on why the surgery is needed and how it connects to the accident is usually the key evidence.

## When a Torn Meniscus Leads to a Knee Replacement

**A torn meniscus at work can lead to a knee replacement months or years later, and Virginia workers’ comp can pay for the replacement if the work injury caused it or sped up the arthritis behind it.** The meniscus cushions the joint. The American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons says meniscus and ligament injuries can make the knee unstable and add wear that, over time, can lead to arthritis ([AAOS](https://www.orthoinfo.org/en/diseases--conditions/arthritis-of-the-knee/)). Arthritis that follows an injury is one of the listed reasons for a total knee replacement ([AAOS](https://www.orthoinfo.org/en/treatment/total-knee-replacement/)).

The path usually looks like this:

1. **The tear.** A twist on a planted foot or a fast rise from a squat. An MRI confirms it.
2. **The first surgery.** Usually arthroscopy, either trimming the torn piece (a partial meniscectomy, about 3 to 6 weeks of recovery) or stitching it (a repair, about 3 to 6 months) ([AAOS](https://www.orthoinfo.org/en/diseases--conditions/meniscus-tears/)).
3. **The knee never quite recovers.** Pain, swelling, and stiffness come back, and later X-rays show arthritis.
4. **The replacement.** Your surgeon recommends a total knee replacement.

### Where Knee Replacement Claims Get Fought

The insurance company’s main argument is almost always the same: the replacement is for age-related arthritis, not the work injury. Expect it if your first MRI mentioned arthritis or called the tear “degenerative.” The legal question is causation. A work accident that aggravates or speeds up a pre-existing condition can still be covered, so the fight comes down to what the doctors say. Your surgeon’s written opinion that the work injury caused or accelerated the need for the replacement is usually the deciding evidence. The insurance company will often get the opposite opinion from its own doctor.

### What a Replacement Changes in Your Claim

| Part of the claim | What changes |
|---|---|
| Medical | If the replacement is reasonable, necessary, and causally related to the accident, the insurance company is required to pay for it and the rehab after it ([Va. Code § 65.2-603](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-603/)). That does not mean it will. |
| Wage checks | Most people resume daily activities within 3 to 6 weeks of a knee replacement ([AAOS](https://www.orthoinfo.org/en/treatment/total-knee-replacement/)), but heavy work takes longer. If you were back at work, wage checks can restart. A claim for them generally must be filed within 24 months from the last day for which you were paid compensation under an award, and checks generally can’t be awarded for more than 90 days before you file ([Va. Code § 65.2-708](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter7/section65.2-708/)). |
| Permanent rating | A replacement often changes the permanent loss of use of your leg. Your doctor rates it once you heal from the replacement. Each 1% of the leg is worth 1.75 weeks of pay, because the leg is scheduled at 175 weeks ([Va. Code § 65.2-503](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter5/section65.2-503/)). A claim for a new or higher rating generally must be filed within 36 months from the last day for which compensation was paid under an award ([Va. Code § 65.2-708](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter7/section65.2-708/)). |
| Settlement | A full and final settlement usually closes your medical benefits for good. Settle before a likely replacement, and workers’ comp will not pay for it. |

## Torn ACL at Work: Surgery, Time Off, and Your Claim

**Virginia workers’ comp covers a torn ACL when a specific accident at work caused it, and a complete tear usually means reconstruction surgery and months away from heavy work.** Complete tears usually need reconstruction because the knee feels unstable, and most people return to full sports in 6 to 12 months ([AAOS](https://www.orthoinfo.org/en/treatment/acl-injury-does-it-require-surgery/)). Heavy work on ladders, docks, and job sites often follows a similar timeline.

### Torn ACL and Meniscus Together

About half of ACL injuries happen along with other damage, such as a meniscus tear, cartilage damage, or another torn ligament ([AAOS](https://www.orthoinfo.org/en/treatment/acl-injury-does-it-require-surgery/)). Two injuries in one knee give the insurance company two chances to dispute something. It may accept the ACL and call the meniscus tear “degenerative,” or the other way around. Make sure every injury is in your medical records and listed on your claim, because the insurance company can refuse to pay for treatment of an injury it says was never part of the claim.

### Can You Work With a Torn ACL?

Some people can, especially in lighter jobs. Orthopedic surgeons say people who do light manual work or sit most of the day may manage a torn ACL without surgery ([AAOS](https://www.orthoinfo.org/en/treatment/acl-injury-does-it-require-surgery/)). In a workers’ comp case, that choice has consequences:

- **Light duty.** If your doctor releases you to light duty and your employer offers a job within your restrictions, refusing it can stop your wage checks ([Va. Code § 65.2-510](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter5/section65.2-510/)).
- **Lower pay.** If the light-duty job pays less than you earned before, you may be owed two-thirds of the difference ([Va. Code § 65.2-502](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter5/section65.2-502/)).
- **Turning down surgery.** Refusing treatment your doctor recommends, without a good reason, can stop your wage checks until you accept the treatment, and you may not get paid for that time ([Va. Code § 65.2-603](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-603/)). Surgeons also note that many people who skip ACL surgery later hurt another part of the knee because it stays unstable ([AAOS](https://www.orthoinfo.org/en/treatment/acl-injury-does-it-require-surgery/)). If you and your surgeon decide against surgery, make sure the reasons are in the records.

## What Is the Average Payout for a Knee Injury at Work in Virginia?

There is no reliable average. Knee claims range from a few weeks of wage checks for a sprain to years of benefits and surgery for a torn ACL or a knee replacement, and the numbers do not average out into anything useful for your case. These are the things that drive what a knee claim is worth:

- **The injury and how it happened.** A clear accident with a structural injury on the MRI is a stronger claim than a gradual problem.
- **Your average weekly wage.** Wage benefits are based on what you earned before the accident.
- **How long you are out of work** or earning less on light duty.
- **Future medical care.** If you will need more treatment or surgery, the insurance company will factor that cost into any settlement.
- **Your permanent impairment rating** for the leg.
- **The insurance company’s defenses.** Insurance companies raise many defenses. A few common ones: the accident did not happen at work, a safety rule was willfully broken, drugs or alcohol caused the accident, or your treatment is not related to the accident.
- **Pre-existing conditions** and how the insurance company’s doctors view them.
- **Your age, training, and the kind of work you can still do.**

You do not have to prove your employer did anything wrong. Your employer’s fault does not matter in workers’ comp. Your own conduct can: willful misconduct, such as knowingly breaking a safety rule, can bar your recovery. That is why the insurance company looks hard at how the accident happened.

### A Note on Functional Capacity Evaluations

Your doctor may send you for a functional capacity evaluation (FCE). It is a test, often several hours long, that measures what you can do: how much weight your knee can support, your range of motion, how you walk, and how long you can stand or kneel. FCE results are often used to set permanent work restrictions and can play a part in the impairment rating.

### Can You Sue Someone Other Than Your Employer?

In almost every case, workers’ comp is your only remedy against your employer ([Va. Code § 65.2-307](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter3/section65.2-307/)). But if someone outside your company caused the injury, such as another driver or the maker of a defective machine, you may also have a separate personal injury case against that person or company. This does not apply to coworkers: you generally cannot sue a coworker who caused your injury. That case runs alongside your workers’ comp claim.

A realistic number depends on all of these at once, and the insurance company runs that math before it makes any offer. Here’s more on [how a Virginia workers’ comp settlement is calculated](/virginia-workers-comp-lawyer/how-to-calculate-a-virginia-workers-comp-settlement/).

## Where Knee Claims Get Fought

Insurance companies often try to pay as little as possible on knee claims, even after accepting them. Common fights include whether the injury is new or degenerative, whether surgery is needed, and what the impairment rating should be. Each fight is decided by medical records, doctors’ opinions, and deadlines most people don’t see coming.

## Knee Injury Questions

### What is the average impairment rating for a total knee replacement?

Virginia has no set or average rating for a knee replacement. A doctor rates the permanent loss of use of your leg after you heal from the surgery, and the rating depends on how well the new knee works. Each 1% of the leg pays 1.75 weeks of compensation ([Va. Code § 65.2-503](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter5/section65.2-503/)), so a 30% rating is 52.5 weeks of pay. The insurance company often sends injured workers to its own doctor, who may give a lower number. Here’s [what happens after an IME in Virginia](/virginia-workers-comp-lawyer/what-happens-after-a-workers-comp-ime-in-virginia/).

### Will workers’ comp pay for a knee replacement years after my injury?

It can, if the replacement is causally related to the work injury and your medical benefits are still open. If you settled your claim full and final, your medical benefits are usually closed for good. Wage checks for the time off after the replacement have their own deadlines, so don’t wait for the surgery date to act.

### Is a torn meniscus at work worth filing a claim?

Yes, file it. Some tears settle down with therapy. Others lead to surgery and, years later, a knee replacement.

A neck injury from an accident at work is covered by Virginia workers’ comp. It can pay for your treatment, including surgery when it is necessary, two-thirds of your average weekly wage while you can’t work, and partial checks if you go back on light duty at lower pay. The neck itself isn’t on Virginia’s list of body parts that get a permanent rating, but a neck injury that leaves lasting weakness or numbness in an arm can lead to a rating for the arm ([Va. Code § 65.2-503](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter5/section65.2-503/)).

## What Virginia Workers’ Comp Pays for a Neck Injury

| Benefit | What it pays | Law |
|---|---|---|
| Medical treatment | Necessary treatment for the injury: doctor visits, imaging, physical therapy, injections, and surgery such as a cervical fusion or disc replacement when it is necessary | [Va. Code § 65.2-603](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-603/) |
| Wage checks while you can’t work (temporary total disability) | Two-thirds of your average weekly wage, up to a weekly maximum. For injuries on or after July 1, 2026, the maximum is $1,507.01 a week. | [Va. Code § 65.2-500](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter5/section65.2-500/) |
| Wage checks on light duty at lower pay (temporary partial disability) | Two-thirds of the difference between what you earned before and what you earn now | [Va. Code § 65.2-502](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter5/section65.2-502/) |
| Permanent partial disability | Not paid for the neck itself. Paid for a rated permanent loss of use of an arm (up to 200 weeks) or hand (up to 150 weeks) caused by the neck injury, in proportion to the rating | [Va. Code § 65.2-503](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter5/section65.2-503/) |
| Permanent and total disability | Lifetime wage checks when an injury to the spinal cord results in total paralysis for all practical purposes, as the Commission decides from the medical evidence, or when the injury causes the loss, or loss of use, of any two of the hands, arms, feet, legs, or eyes (for example, both arms, or one arm and one leg) | [Va. Code § 65.2-503(C)](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter5/section65.2-503/) |

Workers’ comp does not pay for pain and suffering. Wage checks are not paid for the first seven days you are out of work, but if you are out more than three weeks, those first seven days are paid too ([Va. Code § 65.2-509](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter5/section65.2-509/)).

## Common Work Neck Injuries

**Most work neck injuries are one of four kinds, and which one you have shapes the treatment, the time out of work, and how hard the insurance company fights.**

| Injury | What it is | How it usually happens at work |
|---|---|---|
| Neck strain or sprain | Stretched or torn muscles or ligaments | Lifting, pulling, a sudden twist, a vehicle crash |
| Whiplash | A strain from the head snapping forward and back | Rear-end crashes in a work vehicle, being struck by equipment |
| Herniated or bulging cervical disc | Disc material pressing on a nerve, often causing pain, numbness, or weakness down an arm | Heavy lifting, falls, crashes |
| Fracture or spinal cord injury | A broken vertebra or damage to the cord | Falls from height, crashes, being struck by a heavy object |

## Is a Neck Injury at Work Covered?

**A neck injury is covered when it comes from an accident that happened in the course of your job and arose out of it** ([Va. Code § 65.2-101](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter1/section65.2-101/)). In Virginia, an injury by accident means an identifiable incident or sudden precipitating event that causes an obvious sudden mechanical or structural change in the body, at a reasonably definite time (a Supreme Court of Virginia decision involving Southern Express, 257 Va. 181 (1999)). A neck problem that built up gradually, such as stiffness from months of overhead work with no specific incident, usually doesn’t count as an accident in Virginia. A neck that gave out on a specific lift on a specific day does.

Fault generally doesn’t matter. Even if your own mistake caused the injury, you can usually still recover benefits. The exceptions turn on the worker’s own conduct, such as willful misconduct, intoxication, or a willful breach of a reasonable rule the employer made known before the accident, and the employer has the burden of proof ([Va. Code § 65.2-306](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter3/section65.2-306/)).

### Neck Injuries in Work Vehicle Crashes

Many work neck injuries come from crashes while driving for the job. If you were driving as part of your work, the crash is usually covered by comp. If another driver caused it, you may also have a separate claim against that driver, and the employer or its insurer has a lien on that recovery for what comp paid ([Va. Code § 65.2-309](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter3/section65.2-309/)). Comp is generally your only remedy against your employer, and Virginia does not allow these suits against a coworker. ## How Insurers Fight Neck Injury Claims

**The most common fight in a neck claim is over cause: the insurance company says your pain comes from arthritis or “degenerative” changes, not the accident.** By middle age, most people’s neck MRIs show some wear. The insurer may point to that wear and say the accident didn’t cause anything new.

Virginia comp covers an accident that aggravates a condition you already had. The question is whether the work accident caused a new injury or made the old condition worse, and the answer comes from your doctor’s opinion and your records from before and after. Here’s more on [pre-existing conditions and workers’ comp](/pre-existing-conditions-and-workers-comp-claims/).

Other common fights, a few of many:

- **Late symptoms.** Neck pain, and especially whiplash, often gets worse over the first day or two. If the first medical record is days after the accident, the insurer may say something else happened in between.
- **Surgery.** A cervical fusion is expensive, so the insurer may send you to its own doctor, who says you need more therapy first or don’t need surgery at all ([Va. Code § 65.2-607](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-607/)). If it refuses the surgery, you can ask the Commission for a hearing.
- **Light duty.** Once a doctor allows light duty, the insurer may offer a job within your restrictions. Turning down suitable work without a good reason stops your wage checks ([Va. Code § 65.2-510](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter5/section65.2-510/)). Here’s what to know about [light duty in Virginia](/light-duty-workers-comp-virginia/).

## Can You Get a Permanent Rating for a Neck Injury?

**Not for the neck itself, because the neck and back aren’t on Virginia’s schedule of body parts. But if the neck injury causes a permanent loss of use of an arm or hand, that loss can be rated and paid ([Va. Code § 65.2-503](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter5/section65.2-503/)).** This usually happens when a disc or other damage presses on a nerve and leaves lasting weakness, numbness, or loss of grip in the arm.

The rating comes from a doctor once you reach [maximum medical improvement](/virginia-workers-comp-lawyer/what-is-maximum-medical-improvement-in-virginia-workers-comp-claims/). A 15 percent loss of use of an arm works out to 15 percent of 200 weeks, or 30 weeks of pay at your compensation rate. A doctor who rates only “the neck” without addressing the arm may leave out benefits the law allows. Here’s [how impairment ratings work in Virginia](/impairment-rating-virginia-workers-comp/).

## What Is a Neck Injury Claim Worth?

**No one can tell you what a neck injury claim is worth without your medical records and your wages.** What drives the value is whether you need surgery, how long you can’t work, your average weekly wage, any permanent rating for an arm or hand, whether you can go back to your old job, the cost of future care, and how strong the evidence is that the accident caused the injury.

> “On the first call, I won’t guess what your case is worth. I don’t know anything about it yet. Any lawyer who puts a number on it that early is making it up.” (Bryn Swartz)

Most Virginia cases settle with a full and final settlement that closes medical care for good, so a settlement for a neck injury has to account for future treatment, including a possible second surgery at the level above or below a fusion. A settlement is optional; nobody can force you to take one. Here’s more on [workers’ comp settlements in Virginia](/workers-comp-settlement-in-virginia/).

## Steps to Take After a Neck Injury at Work

1. **Report the injury to your employer in writing.** The Virginia Workers’ Compensation Act requires notice to your employer within thirty days of the accident, although exceptions exist ([Va. Code § 65.2-600](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-600/)).
2. **Get medical care right away,** and tell the doctor how the injury happened at work, including any arm or hand symptoms.
3. **File a claim with the Commission,** even if the insurance company is paying. A claim must be on file with the Virginia Workers’ Compensation Commission within two years of your date of accident, although exceptions apply ([Va. Code § 65.2-601](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-601/)). Here’s [how to file a workers’ comp claim](/how-to-file-a-workers-comp-claim/).
4. **List every body part on the claim,** including the neck and any arm with symptoms.
5. **Keep every work note and restriction,** and follow them.

## Neck Injury Questions

### Is Whiplash Covered by Workers’ Comp?

Yes, if it comes from an accident on the job, such as a crash while driving for work. Whiplash is a strain injury, and comp covers it like any other.

### Can I Sue My Employer for a Neck Injury?

Generally no. In Virginia, workers’ comp is the exclusive remedy against your employer for a work injury ([Va. Code § 65.2-307](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter3/section65.2-307/)). You may have a separate claim against someone outside your employer who caused the injury, such as another driver, but not against a coworker.

### My Neck Hurt Before the Accident. Am I Still Covered?

You can be. Comp covers an accident that makes an existing condition worse. The fight is usually over how much of your current problem the accident caused.

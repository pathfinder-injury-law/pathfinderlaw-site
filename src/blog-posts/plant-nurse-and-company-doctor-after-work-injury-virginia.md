*Nothing in this article should be taken as legal advice. Every case is different.*

A visit to the plant nurse is first aid. It is not your choice of doctor. Virginia law requires your employer to provide “a physician chosen by the injured employee from a panel of at least three physicians selected by the employer” ([Va. Code § 65.2-603(A)(1)](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-603/)). If the nurse wraps your hand and sends you back to the line, or the company sends you straight to one clinic it picked, you still have the right to a panel of three physicians and to pick one of them. What gets written down in that first hour, though, can follow your claim for months.

## Is the Plant Nurse My Workers’ Comp Doctor?

**No.** The statute speaks of a panel of “physicians,” and you are the one who chooses from it. A nurse station inside a poultry plant or any other factory can clean a cut, ice a strain, and fill out a first-aid log. But it does not take the place of the panel, and it does not use up your choice.

| Who you see | What that visit is | What to watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Plant nurse or first-aid station | First aid on site | What goes in the log: how it happened, what hurts, and whether it’s called work-related |
| A clinic the company sends you to | Care the employer arranged | Whether you were offered a choice of at least three physicians |
| A doctor you chose from the panel | Your treating physician | Their opinions on work status, restrictions, and surgery carry weight |
| The emergency room | Emergency care | The Commission can order the employer to pay for care in an emergency ([§ 65.2-603(C)](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-603/)) |
| A doctor the employer or insurance company picks for an exam | An exam, not treatment ([§ 65.2-607(A)](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-607/)) | That doctor doesn’t become your doctor |

Our guide to [choosing your workers’ comp doctor in Virginia](/virginia-workers-comp-doctor/) covers the panel rules in depth, including what happens when there is no panel or the claim is denied.

## The Company Sent Me to Its Own Doctor. Do I Have to Go?

**Go to the first visit if you need care, but ask for the panel.** One clinic is not a panel of three. Ask, in writing if you can, for the list of at least three physicians, and write down when you asked and whom you asked.

If your employer never provides a proper panel, the law has an answer. When a doctor other than the one the employer provided treats you “in an emergency or on account of the employer’s failure to provide the medical care,” or “for other good reasons,” the Commission can order the employer to pay the reasonable cost ([§ 65.2-603(C)](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-603/)). Expect a fight over whether a panel was really offered.

If your employer pays all or part of your health coverage, you can also ask whether each doctor on the panel is eligible for payment under it, and the employer has to tell you ([§ 65.2-603(E)](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-603/)).

## What Should I Watch for in the Nurse’s Notes?

**Accuracy. Not silence.** Tell the nurse the truth, and all of it. The trouble is usually what got left out or guessed at:

- **“Not work-related” or “happened at home.”** If it happened on the line, say so plainly, and say exactly what you were doing.
- **“Pre-existing” or “old injury.”** A guess about your history doesn’t belong in a first-aid note. If you had an old problem, here’s how [pre-existing conditions work in Virginia comp](/pre-existing-conditions-and-workers-comp-claims/).
- **Only one body part.** If your wrist and your shoulder both hurt, say both. Pain that shows up later is harder to tie back to the accident.
- **“Declined further care.”** If you were told to go back to work, that’s not the same as refusing a doctor. Ask to see a doctor and make sure the note says you asked.
- **A statement you didn’t write.** Don’t sign a written account of the accident that you don’t agree with.

You can get a copy. Any health care provider attending you has to furnish a copy of a medical report when you ask for it ([Va. Code § 65.2-604](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-604/)). Ask for it while the details are still fresh.

Telling the nurse is also not a substitute for written notice. Virginia law requires written notice of the accident to your employer, and no compensation or medical benefit is payable unless it is given within 30 days, with exceptions ([Va. Code § 65.2-600](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-600/)). Your employer knowing about the accident can matter ([§ 65.2-600(C)](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-600/)), but don’t count on it. If the plant never posted the insurance notice the Commission’s rules require, that may waive the notice defense ([16VAC30-50-80(B)](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title16/agency30/chapter50/section80/)). Put it in writing. Here’s [how to file a workers’ comp claim](/how-to-file-a-workers-comp-claim/).

## Can I Change Doctors Later?

**Not on your own.** Once you choose from the panel, the statute says you “shall accept the attending physician, unless otherwise ordered by the Commission” ([§ 65.2-603(A)(1)](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-603/)). If the insurance company agrees to a change, get that in writing. If it won’t, the Commission decides. So check each doctor’s specialty before you pick. Care your treating doctor deems necessary, such as a referral to a hand surgeon, comes with that doctor (same law).

Refusing the treatment your authorized doctor recommends, without a good reason, can stop your wage checks until the refusal ends ([§ 65.2-603(B)](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-603/)).

## Work Notes and Light Duty After a Plant Injury

**Get every work status in writing from your treating doctor.** Full duty, light duty with specific restrictions, or out of work. A plant nurse saying “you can go back” is not a doctor’s restriction.

If the plant offers light duty that fits your restrictions and you turn it down, you can lose wage benefits while the refusal continues, though your medical care continues ([Va. Code § 65.2-510(A)](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter5/section65.2-510/)). A refusal generally can’t be fixed if it lasts more than six months (same law, subsection C). If the job doesn’t match the doctor’s note, ask the doctor to review it. More on [light duty and refusing light duty in Virginia](/light-duty-workers-comp-virginia/).

## The Surgeons Are Across the Bridge. Who Pays for the Drive?

On the Eastern Shore, an orthopedic or hand surgeon may be an hour or more away, often across the Chesapeake Bay Bridge-Tunnel.

**Reasonable transportation to medical treatment is part of the medical benefit.** In a published 2022 decision, the Court of Appeals of Virginia noted that the Commission “has consistently held that the employer is responsible for the reasonable and necessary transportation in connection with [a] claimant’s medical treatment.” Depending on the circumstances, that can range from mileage to the cost of a taxi or other ride. The court also found nothing in the Act requiring advance notice or pre-approval from the employer before a worker took rides to appointments (a Court of Appeals decision involving Medical Management Intl., 75 Va. App. 679 (2022)).

Distance alone doesn’t settle it. In an unpublished 2024 decision, which isn’t binding, the Court of Appeals affirmed an order making an employer keep paying mileage to a distant authorized doctor. The Full Commission had said reasonableness is “decided by the Commission on a case-by-case basis,” and the court affirmed on res judicata grounds (a decision involving Edelblute’s Service Center, Record No. 1430-23-2 (Va. Ct. App. Aug. 13, 2024) (unpublished)).

1. **Keep a mileage log** for every medical trip: date, where you went, and round-trip miles.
2. **Keep receipts** for rides, tolls, and parking. Whether a particular cost is reasonable is the Commission’s call.
3. **Send the log to the insurance company in writing**, regularly, and keep a copy.
4. **If it won’t pay,** you can ask the Commission to decide.

The Commission uses a standard rate per mile. We haven’t listed a number here because it changes. Ask the adjuster for the current rate in writing.

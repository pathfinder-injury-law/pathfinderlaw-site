If you were hurt at a poultry plant in Virginia, workers’ compensation can pay for your medical care and part of your lost wages, no matter whose fault the accident was. A knife cut on the line, a fall on a wet floor, a burn, a chemical splash, a hand caught in a machine, a strained back from lifting, or a forklift hitting you can all be covered. Two deadlines matter most: give your employer written notice of the accident within 30 days ([Va. Code § 65.2-600](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-600/)), and file your own claim with the Virginia Workers’ Compensation Commission within two years of the accident ([Va. Code § 65.2-601](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-601/)).

## What Poultry Plant Injuries Does Workers’ Comp Cover?

**Virginia’s law covers an “injury by accident arising out of and in the course of the employment”** ([Va. Code § 65.2-101](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter1/section65.2-101/)). For a plant worker, that usually means a specific thing that happened on a specific shift. The injuries we see from the large poultry plants on the Shore include:

- **Cuts and amputations** from knives, saws, deboning lines, and conveyor equipment
- **Falls** on floors that stay wet, greasy, or icy in the cold rooms
- **Burns** from scalders, hot water, steam, and cleaning chemicals
- **Chemical splashes** to the eyes and skin during sanitation
- **Crush injuries** from machines, carts, pallets, and doors
- **Lifting injuries** to the back, shoulder, or knee from moving boxes, tubs, and product
- **Forklift and pallet jack incidents**, inside the plant or on the loading dock

The hard part is usually not whether the injury is serious. It’s proving it happened at work, at a particular moment, doing a particular task. A back that “just started hurting” over a few weeks can be a much harder claim to tie to work than a back that gave out when you lifted a full tub at 2 a.m. Write down the date, the time, the task, and who was nearby while you still remember.

## How Do I Report a Poultry Plant Injury?

**Tell a supervisor right away, then put it in writing.** The law asks for written notice “immediately” or as soon as practicable, and says no compensation or medical benefits are payable unless written notice is given within 30 days, unless you have a reasonable excuse and the employer wasn’t harmed by the delay ([Va. Code § 65.2-600](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-600/)). The notice should give your name and address, the time and place of the accident, and what happened and what got hurt.

Reporting to the plant is not the same as filing a claim. The employer’s accident report is the employer’s paperwork. Your claim with the Commission is a separate form that you file yourself, and if it isn’t filed within two years, your right to compensation is “forever barred” ([Va. Code § 65.2-601](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-601/)). A few situations, such as the employer paying wages or furnishing medical care, can extend that deadline ([Va. Code § 65.2-602](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-602/)), but don’t count on one. Plenty of workers find out too late that the insurer paying some bills never meant a claim was on file.

If you came to the plant through a staffing agency or a contract sanitation crew, report to both your agency and the plant. Here’s more on [staffing agency and sanitation crew injuries at a poultry plant](/staffing-agency-or-sanitation-crew-injured-at-poultry-plant/).

## What Will the Plant Nurse and the Insurance Company Ask?

**Expect questions about how it happened, whether you followed the rules, and whether you were hurt before.** Most large plants have a nurse or first aid station, and that visit is often the first record of your injury. What you say there gets written down and sent to the insurer. Be accurate and complete: name every body part that hurts, even the ones that seem minor today.

The insurance adjuster will usually call and ask for a recorded statement. Common questions include:

- Exactly what you were doing, and whether it was your normal job
- Whether you were wearing your gloves, mesh, goggles, or other safety gear
- Whether you knew about the safety rule for that task
- Whether you’ve ever hurt that body part before
- Whether anyone saw it happen

Those safety questions have a purpose. Virginia law bars compensation for an injury caused by a worker’s willful breach of a reasonable safety rule the employer made known to the worker, or a willful failure or refusal to use a safety appliance or perform a duty required by statute. The employer has the burden to prove that defense ([Va. Code § 65.2-306](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter3/section65.2-306/)). The employer has to prove you actually knew the rule and knowingly did the forbidden act, and a rule that isn’t enforced in practice is a weak defense. But a careless answer on a recorded call can hand the insurer that argument. Read about [the insurance company’s recorded statement](/insurance-recorded-statement/) before you give one, and see our guide to [the plant nurse and company doctor after a work injury](/plant-nurse-and-company-doctor-after-work-injury-virginia/).

## Who Picks My Doctor and Pays My Medical Bills?

**The employer must give you a panel of at least three doctors, and you choose one** ([Va. Code § 65.2-603](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-603/)). Medical care for the injury is owed as long as it’s necessary, at no cost to you. For an amputation or loss of use of a hand, arm, foot, or leg, the law also requires the employer to provide prosthetics and the training to use them (same law). Our guide to [choosing a workers’ comp doctor in Virginia](/virginia-workers-comp-doctor/) explains how the panel works and what happens if you see someone else.

## When Do Wage Checks Start?

**If your doctor keeps you out of work for more than seven days, you can be paid two-thirds of your average weekly wage, within state limits** ([Va. Code § 65.2-500](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter5/section65.2-500/)). The first seven days are unpaid, but if you’re out more than three weeks, you’re paid back to the first day ([Va. Code § 65.2-509](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter5/section65.2-509/)). Overtime and second jobs can affect your average weekly wage, so keep your pay stubs.

If a cut or crush injury leaves you without part of a finger or hand, or a burn leaves a bad scar, the law sets separate payments for permanent loss and for severe disfigurement ([Va. Code § 65.2-503](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter5/section65.2-503/)). What drives those payments is the body part, how much use you lost, and your average weekly wage. ## Steps to Take After a Poultry Plant Injury

| Step | When | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Tell your supervisor and go to the nurse or a doctor | Right away | Creates the first record that you were hurt at work |
| Give written notice of the accident | Within 30 days | Required for compensation and medical benefits ([§ 65.2-600](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-600/)) |
| Write down the date, time, task, and witnesses | The same day | Shift details fade fast and the insurer will ask |
| Pick a doctor from the employer’s panel | Before ongoing treatment | Keeps your medical bills covered ([§ 65.2-603](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-603/)) |
| Get your work restrictions in writing | At every visit | Wage checks turn on what your doctor says you can do |
| File your claim with the Commission | Well before two years | Otherwise the right to compensation is lost ([§ 65.2-601](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-601/)) |

## Common Questions From Poultry Plant Workers

### Does It Matter Whose Fault the Accident Was?

Usually not. Workers’ comp doesn’t require you to prove the plant did something wrong. The main fault-based defenses are the ones in [§ 65.2-306](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter3/section65.2-306/), such as willful misconduct or intoxication, and the employer has to prove them.

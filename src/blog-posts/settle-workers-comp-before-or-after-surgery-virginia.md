*Nothing in this article should be taken as legal advice. Every case is different.*

In Virginia, nobody can make you settle a workers’ comp claim, before surgery or after it. A settlement is a voluntary agreement, and the Workers’ Compensation Commission approves it only if it’s clearly in your best interests ([Va. Code § 65.2-701](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter7/section65.2-701/)). The problem with settling before surgery is that the biggest unknowns in the claim are still unknown: whether the surgery works, how long you’ll be out, whether you’ll need more treatment, and what permanent loss you’re left with. A full and final settlement closes the insurer’s duty to pay for that future care, so the price has to cover it.

## What Changes After Surgery

**Most of the facts that set a claim’s value only show up after surgery and recovery.**

| What’s unknown before surgery | What surgery and recovery tell you |
|---|---|
| Whether the surgery fixes the problem | The result, and whether more surgery is likely |
| How long you’ll be out of work | Actual weeks of temporary total disability benefits ([Va. Code § 65.2-500](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter5/section65.2-500/)) |
| Your permanent work restrictions | What jobs you can return to, and at what pay |
| Your impairment rating | Benefits for permanent loss of use ([Va. Code § 65.2-503](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter5/section65.2-503/)) wait until the injury reaches maximum medical improvement. While surgery is still on the table, that point usually can’t be known yet (a Supreme Court of Virginia decision involving Spotsylvania County, 218 Va. 565 (1977)). |
| Future medical costs | Hardware removal, injections, therapy, prescriptions, and joint replacement down the road |

An insurer’s offer before surgery is a guess about all of that. The insurer has adjusters, nurses, and records reviewers making its guess. You are usually guessing alone.

## What a Settlement Closes

**A full and final settlement, once the Commission approves it, generally ends the insurer’s obligation for everything the settlement covers, including future medical care for that injury.** After that, comp no longer pays for the surgery you didn’t have yet, or the next one.

Virginia settlements don’t all look the same:

- **Full and final.** One lump sum that closes wage benefits and medical care. You can’t come back for more.
- **Wages settled, medical left open.** The settlement pays a lump sum for wage benefits, and the insurer stays responsible for causally related medical care under the usual rules ([Va. Code § 65.2-603](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-603/)). Whether an insurer will agree to this varies.

## Why an Insurer May Offer Before Surgery

**Surgery is expensive, and so are the months of wage benefits that follow it.** A settlement before surgery lets the insurer close both for a known number. That doesn’t make every early offer a bad one, but it explains the timing.

- **The surgery is approved but not scheduled.** The insurer is required to pay for reasonable, necessary, causally related treatment ([§ 65.2-603](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-603/)). Approval doesn’t mean it will keep paying everything that follows without a fight.
- **The surgery was denied after an IME or a records review.** A denial isn’t the last word. You can ask the Commission for a hearing on the medical dispute. Here’s [what happens after an IME](/virginia-workers-comp-lawyer/what-happens-after-a-workers-comp-ime-in-virginia/).
- **You want to be done.** Wanting out of the comp system is a real reason. It should be weighed against what the surgery and recovery may cost you later without comp paying for them.

## When Settling Before Surgery Comes Up

**There are situations where settling before surgery is on the table, and each has trade-offs that turn on your facts.**

| Situation | What to weigh |
|---|---|
| You don’t want the surgery | Without the surgery, the rating and restrictions may be fixed now. But refusing medical care the insurer provides can suspend your wage benefits until the refusal ends, and those weeks aren’t paid back, unless the Commission finds the refusal was justified ([Va. Code § 65.2-603(B)](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-603/)). |
| You have other health insurance | Health insurers often exclude work injuries. Find out in writing before you count on it. |
| You’re on Medicare, or reasonably expect to be within 30 months | If the settlement closes your medical care, Medicare’s interests have to be considered. A [Medicare set-aside](/workers-comp-settlement-in-virginia/#medicare) comes up only when Medicare’s interest must be protected. Not every settlement has one. |
| The insurer disputes that the injury caused the need for surgery | The dispute itself is a risk on both sides, and that risk is part of the price. |

## What Drives the Value Either Way

> “On the first call, I won’t guess what your case is worth. I don’t know anything about it yet. Any lawyer who puts a number on it that early is making it up.” (Bryn Swartz)

There isn’t an honest average for a settlement before or after surgery. What drives the number is the [average weekly wage](/virginia-workers-comp-lawyer/how-is-workers-comp-calculated-in-virginia/) behind your checks, how many weeks of benefits remain, the impairment rating, the cost of future medical care, and how strong the insurer’s defenses are.

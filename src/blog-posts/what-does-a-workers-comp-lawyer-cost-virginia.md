*Nothing in this article should be taken as legal advice. Every case is different.*

Most Virginia workers’ comp lawyers charge nothing up front. The fee is usually a percentage of the money the lawyer recovers for you, and in a Virginia comp case the Workers’ Compensation Commission has to approve that fee before the lawyer can be paid ([Va. Code § 65.2-714](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter7/section65.2-714/)). The Commission can cut a fee it finds too high and can order a lawyer to pay back a fee that was excessive. The Commission also says it does not set fees by a schedule or a percentage. It looks at the time spent, the effort, the kind of work, and the results ([Commission page for attorneys](https://workcomp.virginia.gov/content/attorneys)). That makes comp fees different from almost any other kind of legal work.

## How Workers’ Comp Lawyers Are Paid in Virginia

**A comp lawyer is almost always paid on a contingency: a share of what the lawyer gets for you, approved by the Commission.**

| Question | Short answer | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Do I pay anything up front? | Usually not. Ask before you sign. | The fee agreement |
| Who decides the fee? | The Commission has the final say on every fee in a comp case | [§ 65.2-714(A)](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter7/section65.2-714/) |
| Can the Commission lower a fee? | Yes, and it can order an excessive fee paid back | [§ 65.2-714(A)](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter7/section65.2-714/) |
| Does the agreement have to be in writing? | A contingent fee agreement has to be in writing and say how the fee is figured | [Va. Rule of Professional Conduct 1.5(c)](https://vsb.org/Site/Site/about/rules-regulations/rpc-part6-sec2.aspx) |
| Who handles fee disputes? | The Commission, not a local court. Appeals go to the Full Commission, then the Court of Appeals of Virginia | [§ 65.2-714(A)](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter7/section65.2-714/), [§ 65.2-706](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter7/section65.2-706/) |

The fee comes out of the benefits the lawyer recovers, such as back pay for weeks you couldn’t work or a settlement. If the lawyer recovers nothing, a contingency fee is nothing. Read the agreement for how costs are handled, because that is a separate question from the fee.

## What the Fee Comes Out Of

**The question that matters more than the percentage is what the percentage is taken from.** Two agreements with the same percentage can cost you very different amounts.

- **Weekly checks the insurer was already paying.** If the insurance company accepted your claim and was paying before you hired anyone, ask whether the lawyer takes a share of those checks. Many lawyers don’t.
- **Back pay the lawyer wins.** When the insurer denied the claim or stopped your checks, the fee usually comes from the past-due benefits the Commission awards.
- **A settlement.** The fee is part of the settlement paperwork, and the deputy commissioner who approves the settlement also approves the fee.
- **Medical bills.** In a contested claim that the Commission finds compensable after a hearing, or after the insurer drops its defense, the Commission awards your lawyer a reasonable fee paid out of the money the insurer pays to doctors and hospitals (or to a health insurer that paid those bills first), not out of your pocket ([§ 65.2-714(B)](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter7/section65.2-714/)).

## Costs Are Not the Same as Fees

**Costs are the out-of-pocket expenses of building your case, and the fee agreement should say who pays them.** Common costs in a Virginia comp case:

| Cost | What it is |
|---|---|
| Medical records | Hospitals and doctors charge to copy records |
| Doctor’s opinion letters or depositions | Many doctors charge for written opinions on what caused your injury or what you can do |
| Court reporter and transcripts | For depositions and some hearings |
| Filing a claim or asking for a hearing | The Commission’s claim form lists no filing fee, and the Commission does not charge a fee for hearings |

Ask whether costs come out of your share or the lawyer’s, and whether you owe them if there is no recovery. When an employer or insurer brings or defends a proceeding without reasonable grounds, or delays payment without reasonable grounds, the Commission can make the employer or insurer pay the whole cost of the proceeding, including a reasonable attorney’s fee ([Va. Code § 65.2-713](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter7/section65.2-713/)).

## Why the Cheapest Choice Isn’t Always No Lawyer

**A lawyer’s fee is easy to see. The cost of a claim that goes wrong is not.** Here are a few of the places where an unrepresented claim loses money in Virginia:

- **A missed deadline.** A claim not filed with the Commission within two years of the accident is generally barred for good ([Va. Code § 65.2-601](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-601/)), even if the insurer was paying. See [the Virginia workers’ comp deadlines](/workers-comp-deadlines-virginia/).
- **A wrong average weekly wage.** The weekly check for total disability is two-thirds of your average weekly wage, up to a state maximum ([Va. Code § 65.2-500](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter5/section65.2-500/)). Leave out overtime and every check is short, for as long as the checks run.
- **A low impairment rating.** A few percentage points on a rating can mean weeks of pay. Here’s [how impairment ratings work in Virginia](/impairment-rating-virginia-workers-comp/).
- **A settlement that closes medical care.** A full and final settlement, once approved, ends the insurer’s duty to pay for that injury’s treatment. See [workers’ comp settlements in Virginia](/workers-comp-settlement-in-virginia/).

Here’s more on [where Virginia comp claims go wrong without a lawyer](/do-i-need-a-lawyer-for-workers-comp-virginia/).

## Questions to Ask About the Fee Before You Sign

1. What percentage do you charge, and what is it taken from?
2. Do you take a fee from checks the insurer was already paying when I hired you?
3. How are costs handled, and do I owe them if we recover nothing?
4. Is any of the fee paid from medical bills instead of my benefits?
5. Will I see the fee in writing before the Commission approves it?

Here are more [questions to ask a workers’ comp lawyer](/questions-to-ask-a-workers-comp-lawyer/).

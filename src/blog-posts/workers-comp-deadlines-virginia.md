*Nothing in this article should be taken as legal advice. Every case is different.*

In Virginia, you must give your employer written notice of a work accident within 30 days, and you must file a claim with the Virginia Workers’ Compensation Commission within two years of the accident ([Va. Code §§ 65.2-600](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-600/) and [65.2-601](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-601/)). Miss the two-year deadline, and the right to benefits is “forever barred.” Several other deadlines follow once a claim is underway: 30 days to ask for review of a decision, 24 months to reopen an award for a change in condition, and short windows to answer the insurance company’s filings.

## Virginia Workers’ Comp Deadlines at a Glance

**Most of these deadlines can’t be extended, and the Commission has no power to excuse a missed one simply because it seems unfair.**

| What | Deadline | Counted from | Law |
|---|---|---|---|
| Written notice to your employer | 30 days, unless the Commission accepts a reasonable excuse and the employer wasn’t prejudiced | The accident | [§ 65.2-600](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-600/) |
| Claim filed with the Commission (injury by accident) | 2 years | The accident | [§ 65.2-601](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-601/) |
| Claim for most occupational diseases | 2 years after a diagnosis is first communicated to you, or 5 years after your last harmful exposure at work, whichever comes first | The diagnosis or the last exposure | [§ 65.2-406](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter4/section65.2-406/) |
| Death benefits claim | 2 years from the death, and a claim must also have been filed within 2 years of the accident | The death and the accident | [§ 65.2-601](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-601/) |
| Asking for more wage benefits on a change in condition | 24 months (36 for a permanent partial disability rating) | The last day for which compensation was paid under an award | [§ 65.2-708](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter7/section65.2-708/) |
| Asking the full Commission to review a deputy commissioner’s decision | 30 days | The date of the decision | [§ 65.2-705](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter7/section65.2-705/) |
| Appeal to the Court of Appeals of Virginia | 30 days, by notice of appeal filed with the Commission’s clerk | The date of the full Commission’s decision | [§ 65.2-706](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter7/section65.2-706/) |

Some occupational diseases, such as certain cancers and lung diseases, have their own periods in [§ 65.2-406](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter4/section65.2-406/). If a worker dies of an occupational disease within the filing period, the family has three years after the death to file ([§ 65.2-406(B)](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter4/section65.2-406/)).

## How Long Do You Have to Report a Work Injury in Virginia?

**Thirty days, in writing.** The notice should give your name and address, the time and place of the accident, and what happened and what was hurt ([§ 65.2-600(B)](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-600/)). Telling your supervisor out loud can count if the employer knew about the accident, but it is much harder to prove later. Put it in writing, list every body part that hurts, and keep a copy.

Late notice doesn’t automatically end a claim. The Commission can accept a reasonable excuse if the employer wasn’t prejudiced by the delay. But until notice is given, you generally can’t recover doctor bills or wage benefits for that earlier period unless the employer already knew ([§ 65.2-600(C) and (D)](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-600/)).

## How Long Do You Have to File a Workers’ Comp Claim in Virginia?

**Two years from the date of the accident.** The statute says the right to compensation “shall be forever barred” unless a claim is filed with the Commission in that time ([§ 65.2-601](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-601/)).

Three things people assume count as filing, and don’t:

1. **Telling your employer.** That is notice, a separate requirement.
2. **Your employer reporting the accident.** The employer’s first report goes to the Commission, but it is not your claim.
3. **The insurance company accepting the injury and paying.** Payments are voluntary until there is an award. The insurer can stop at any time. Its payments can pause the two-year clock in some cases, but the rules are narrow, so don’t count on them.

File your own claim with the Commission even if the insurance company is paying. That is what protects the deadline. Here’s [how to file a workers’ comp claim](/how-to-file-a-workers-comp-claim/).

## Does the Claim Cover Every Injury?

**Usually, only the injuries it lists.** The Supreme Court of Virginia has held that, within the filing period, a worker must claim every injury that grows out of the accident (a case against Shea-Ball Construction Co., 216 Va. 442, 446 (1975)). The Court of Appeals of Virginia applied that rule this year in a case involving Food Lion, No. 0117-25-2 (Va. Ct. App. Aug. 18, 2026). The worker’s timely claim covered his shoulder. His doctors diagnosed and treated his neck within the two years, but he never claimed it, so the neck claim was too late. That decision is unpublished, so it is not binding precedent, though courts can cite it as informative. There is a narrow exception when doctors miss the true source of an injury until after the deadline (a case involving Corporate Resource Management, 51 Va. App. 118 (2008) (en banc)), but don’t plan around it. If a second body part starts hurting, or a doctor finds another injury, add it to the claim before two years pass. ## How Is the Deadline Counted?

**The day of the accident doesn’t count, so the two-year deadline falls on the anniversary of the accident. A deadline that lands on a weekend, a legal holiday, or a day the clerk’s office is closed moves to the next business day** ([Va. Code § 1-210](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title1/chapter2/section1-210/)). In one case, against Goodwill of Central and Coastal Virginia (Sept. 17, 2026), the full Commission ruled a claim timely when the two-year date fell on a Sunday and the next day was Veterans Day, so the claim filed that Tuesday was on time. That case went back to the deputy commissioner for a decision on the merits, so it is not final. That claim was mailed on a Saturday, but it counted as filed only when it reached the Commission. A claim sent by certified or registered mail counts as filed the day it is mailed, while regular mail, email, and fax count only when they arrive ([Va. Code § 65.2-101](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter1/section65.2-101/)).

That rule saves a day or two, not weeks. Treat the real deadline as well before the last day.

## Can Anything Pause the Two-Year Deadline?

**Sometimes, but the exceptions are narrow and fact-heavy.** Under [Va. Code § 65.2-602](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-602/), the deadline can be paused (tolled) when the employer pays you compensation or wages during a period you can’t work, furnishes medical care under its comp coverage, or fails to file its first report of the accident with the Commission. The pause has limits, and payments made after the deadline has already run don’t revive it.

The Commission can also stop an employer from using the deadline when the employer’s own conduct kept the worker from filing. The Supreme Court of Virginia has said that takes fraud or concealment by the employer, or an act reasonably calculated to induce the worker not to file (a case against United Airlines, 223 Va. 197 (1982)). The worker carries that burden, and it depends on proof about who said what and when. A claim filed on time never needs one.

## Deadlines After You File

**Filing starts a new set of clocks.**

- **The insurance company’s answer.** After you file, the Commission can order the insurer to say whether it accepts or denies the claim. Here’s [what happens when you file a workers’ comp claim](/what-happens-when-you-file-a-workers-comp-claim/).
- **A request to stop your checks.** Under an award, the insurer has to file an application with the Commission, and you have up to 15 days from the date it was filed to send the Commission your evidence against it ([Commission Rule 1, 16VAC30-50-20(E)(3)](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title16/agency30/chapter50/section20/)).
- **A decision you disagree with.** You have 30 days to ask the full Commission to review it ([§ 65.2-705](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter7/section65.2-705/)). Here’s [how to appeal a workers’ comp denial in Virginia](/how-to-appeal-a-workers-comp-denial-in-virginia/).
- **An injury that gets worse after checks stop.** The change-in-condition deadline runs from the last day you were paid under an award. Here’s [how long a workers’ comp claim can stay open](/how-long-can-a-workers-comp-claim-stay-open/).

## Workers’ Comp Deadline Questions

### Is the Deadline Different if My Employer Never Reported the Accident?

It can be. An employer’s failure to file its first report with the Commission is one of the situations that can pause the deadline under [§ 65.2-602](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-602/). Don’t count on it. File within two years.

### What if I Didn’t Know I Had a Claim?

For an injury by accident, the two years run from the accident, not from when you learned you could file. For most occupational diseases, the clock starts when a diagnosis is first communicated to you, with an outside limit of five years from your last harmful exposure ([§ 65.2-406](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter4/section65.2-406/)).

*Nothing in this article should be taken as legal advice. Every case is different.*

When a work accident causes a worker’s death in Virginia within nine years, the worker’s dependents receive weekly death benefits of two-thirds of the worker’s average weekly wage, for 500 weeks from the date of injury for a dependent spouse and children, or 400 weeks for other dependents ([Va. Code § 65.2-512](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter5/section65.2-512/)). The employer also pays burial expenses of up to $15,000 and up to $1,000 to transport the deceased. A claim for death benefits has to be filed with the Virginia Workers’ Compensation Commission within two years of the death, and a claim must also have been filed within two years of the accident ([Va. Code § 65.2-601](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-601/)).

## What Virginia Workers’ Comp Death Benefits Include

**Death benefits replace part of the income the family lost and cover burial costs. They don’t include pain and suffering or other damages.**

| Benefit | Amount | Law |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly payments to dependents | 66⅔ percent of the worker’s average weekly wage, no more than 100 percent and no less than 25 percent of Virginia’s statewide average weekly wage. For accidents from July 1, 2026, through June 30, 2027, the maximum is $1,507.01 a week ([Commission rate notice](https://workcomp.virginia.gov/news/notice-of-2026-rates)). | [§ 65.2-512(A)](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter5/section65.2-512/) |
| How long weekly payments last | 500 weeks from the date of injury for a spouse or children presumed wholly dependent; 400 weeks for others | [§ 65.2-512(A)](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter5/section65.2-512/) |
| Burial expenses | Up to $15,000 | [§ 65.2-512(B)](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter5/section65.2-512/) |
| Transportation of the deceased | Up to $1,000 | [§ 65.2-512(B)](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter5/section65.2-512/) |
| Medical bills from the injury before death | Covered as in any comp claim | [§ 65.2-603](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-603/) |

Starting January 1, 2028, the Commission will adjust the burial and transportation limits each year for inflation ([§ 65.2-512(B)](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter5/section65.2-512/)). Some older pages online still show a lower funeral limit. The current statute says $15,000.

The weekly amount is figured from the worker’s average weekly wage, generally the worker’s earnings in the 52 weeks before the injury ([Va. Code § 65.2-101](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter1/section65.2-101/)). Overtime, raises, and short or irregular work histories can all change that figure, and the insurance company’s number is not always right. Pay stubs, tax returns, and the employer’s payroll records matter.

## Who Gets Workers’ Comp Death Benefits in Virginia?

**Virginia law presumes some family members were wholly dependent on the worker, and those people come first** ([Va. Code § 65.2-515](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter5/section65.2-515/)).

| Who | When they qualify | How long |
|---|---|---|
| Spouse | Was living with the worker, or had not voluntarily deserted the worker, at the time of the accident, and was actually dependent on the worker | 500 weeks, shared with dependent children |
| Children under 18 | Always presumed dependent | 500 weeks, shared, until they turn 18 |
| Children 18 or older who can’t earn a living | Physically or mentally unable to support themselves | 500 weeks, shared |
| Children under 23 in school full time | Enrolled full time in an accredited school | 500 weeks, shared, while they qualify |
| Parents in destitute circumstances | Only if there is no spouse or child who qualifies above | 400 weeks, shared with anyone else who was wholly dependent in fact |
| Others who were wholly dependent in fact | Only if there is no spouse or child who qualifies above; they share with any parents in destitute circumstances | 400 weeks, shared |
| Partial dependents | Only if there are no total dependents at all | 400 weeks, split by how much each relied on the worker |

“Child” includes stepchildren, adopted children, children born after the worker’s death, and acknowledged children born outside marriage, but not a married child ([§ 65.2-515(B)](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter5/section65.2-515/)). Anyone claiming dependency in fact, rather than by presumption, has to show the dependency existed for at least three months before the accident ([Va. Code § 65.2-516](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter5/section65.2-516/)).

## How the Benefits Are Split and When They End

**Total dependents share the weekly benefit equally, and partial dependents get nothing while any total dependent qualifies** ([§ 65.2-512(C)](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter5/section65.2-512/)).

- **When one dependent’s share ends,** it is divided among the remaining dependents in the same group ([§ 65.2-512(D)](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter5/section65.2-512/)).
- **A spouse’s dependency ends on death or remarriage,** and that share goes to the children or other dependents ([Va. Code § 65.2-517](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter5/section65.2-517/)).
- **A child’s dependency generally ends at 18,** unless the child can’t earn a living or is a full-time student under 23.
- **The 500 or 400 weeks run from the date of injury, not the date of death.** If the worker was paid weekly benefits before dying, the death benefits start from the last of those payments and still end at the same point ([§ 65.2-512(E)](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter5/section65.2-512/)). ## What the Family Has to Prove

**The death has to result from a work accident that would have been covered if the worker had lived.** The same rules apply as in any Virginia comp claim: the injury has to arise out of and in the course of the employment. Here’s [how to qualify for workers’ comp in Virginia](/how-to-qualify-for-workers-comp-in-virginia/).

Some deaths are obvious, such as a fall from a roof or a crush injury. Others are not. When a worker dies weeks or years after the accident, from a complication, an infection, or a medication, the question becomes whether the work injury caused the death. That fight is usually about medical records and a doctor’s opinion on cause of death. The statute also limits benefits to deaths within nine years of the accident ([§ 65.2-512(A)](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter5/section65.2-512/)).

## Deadlines for a Death Benefits Claim

**Two deadlines apply, and both have to be met** ([Va. Code § 65.2-601](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-601/)):

1. **A claim filed within two years of the accident.** If the worker filed a claim before dying, that usually covers this part.
2. **A claim for death benefits filed within two years of the death.**

Written notice to the employer is also required within 30 days of the accident or the death, unless the Commission accepts a reasonable excuse and is satisfied the employer was not prejudiced by the delay ([Va. Code § 65.2-600(D)](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter6/section65.2-600/)). If a worker dies more than two years after an accident and never filed a claim, the family’s claim may already be barred. ## Steps for a Family After a Work Death

1. **Get the death certificate and any autopsy report.** Cause of death is often the central question.
2. **Find out whether the worker filed a claim.** Ask the employer for its insurance company’s name and the claim number, and check with the Commission.
3. **Gather proof of dependency.** Marriage and birth certificates, school enrollment for children 18 to 22, and proof of support for anyone else.
4. **Gather proof of earnings.** Pay stubs, W-2s, and tax returns for the year before the accident.
5. **Keep the funeral and transportation bills.** The employer pays up to the limits above.
6. **File the claim for death benefits with the Commission** within two years of the death.
